=======================================================================
BACKGROUND (established, do not re-litigate)
=======================================================================

Two independent error sources degrade off-momentum ACD reconstruction:

1. TRUNCATION ERROR. The dispersive closed orbit is modelled first-order as
   pt*D. The neglected pt^2*D2 term is a constant per-BPM offset scaling as pt^2
   (px residual 1.4e-7 / 1.3e-6 / 1.4e-5 rad at dp = 1e-3 / 3e-3 / 1e-2 on PSB
   ring 3). Already addressed by the opt-in
   `ACDipoleConfig.dispersive_closed_orbit=True`, which references the exact
   MAD-NG orbit at pt and sets the betatron-stage pt to zero. Restores
   px_downstream R^2 from 0.9836 to 0.999999 -- BUT ONLY when the model carries
   the machine's magnet errors.

2. MODEL ERROR. When the model does not know the machine's magnet errors, the
   reconstruction collapses (6 mm model/machine CO mismatch, px_downstream R^2
   NEGATIVE). The pt method is then irrelevant. The designated remedy, the
   data-mean closed orbit, is algebraically a NO-OP for the BPM momenta:
   `_closed_orbit_momenta` infers the CO angle with the same linear neighbour-pair
   operator used to reconstruct the momenta, so removing the orbit and restoring
   its angle cancels identically. Verified numerically (bit-identical R^2).

The kick fit (dpx/dpy R^2) is BLIND to all of this -- it stays at 0.999999
because a harmonic fit absorbs a constant into its offset parameter. Validate on
the absolute BPM momenta (px_bpm_upstream / px_bpm_downstream vs tracked truth),
never on kick-fit quality.

=======================================================================
THE INVESTIGATION
=======================================================================

The scenario is a REAL PSB measurement: the machine has both bend field errors
and quadrupole gradient errors, and the model knows NEITHER a priori. The
operational handle we do have is that the closed orbit can be measured at
several different delta_p values.

--- Part A: estimate the bend errors from closed orbits at different delta_p ---

Build a PSB ring-3 setup with bend errors AND quad gradient errors applied to the
tracking line only (`apply_bend_errors_to_model=False`,
`apply_quad_errors_to_model=False` in tests/psb_tracking.py -- both flags exist).

Measure the closed orbit at a set of delta_p values (choose a sensible spread,
e.g. 0, +/-1e-3, +/-3e-3, +/-8e-3; the CO is the per-BPM turn-mean of the ACD
flat-top data, or track a single particle without excitation if that is cleaner
and faster -- justify whichever you pick).

Fit the bend errors from that family of orbits. The physics: a dipole field error
produces an orbit distortion that is (to first order) independent of delta_p,
while the dispersive orbit scales with pt. Measuring at several delta_p therefore
separates the two. Decide and document the estimator you use -- a linear response
matrix from MAD-NG (orbit response to each bend's k0) inverted with SVD is the
obvious route; `tmom_recon/svd.py` exists and may already have machinery.

Report honestly: how well are the individual bend k0 errors recovered, and how
well is the resulting MODEL closed orbit matched to the machine's? The second
number is what actually matters. Also check what the un-modelled QUAD errors do
to this estimate -- they are a systematic the bend fit cannot absorb, and
quantifying that contamination is a key result.

--- Part B: is the truncation fix still worth anything? ---

With the model improved by the Part A bend-error estimate (quads still unknown),
re-run the comparison from NOTES_offmomentum_closed_orbit.md section 3:

  - linear pt*D    vs    dispersive_closed_orbit=True (exact CO)

at several delta_p. The question the user is asking: once the dominant model
error is reduced, does the pt^2*D2 truncation become the limiting term again, or
is it still buried under residual model error? I.e. IS WHAT WE HAVE ENOUGH?

Produce the crossover: at what level of residual model CO error does the choice
of pt method start to matter? Express it as a curve or table, not a single point.

--- Part C: second-order dispersion ---

MAD-NG twiss with `chrom=true` provides `ddx`, `ddpx`, `ddy`, `ddpy` -- the
chromatic derivatives of the dispersion functions (confirmed in
MADdocs/refman/source/mad_cmd_twiss.rst). It also provides `dmu1`/`dmu2`
(chromatic phase derivatives) and `wx`/`phix` (Montague functions).

Determine empirically:

  1. The exact convention/normalisation. Is the second-order orbit
     pt*dx + 0.5*pt^2*ddx, or pt*dx + pt^2*ddx, and is ddx per unit pt or per
     unit delta? (Recall MAD dx/dpx are per unit PT, not per delta -- PSB
     beta~0.52 at 160 MeV kinetic, so a 1/beta factor is a real ~1.92x trap
     here.) Fit it against the exact MAD-NG off-momentum CO and state the
     convention you established, with the residual.

  2. Whether a second-order closed-orbit model (pt*D + second-order term) is a
     useful THIRD method sitting between "linear pt*D" and "exact CO". It would
     matter in the case where you have good dispersion knowledge but cannot
     trust the model's closed orbit -- e.g. combined with a MEASURED dispersion.
     Quantify the residual it leaves vs the exact CO.

  3. Whether the chromatic optics terms (dmu1, wx/phix) explain and can correct
     the smaller LINEAR-in-pt gain error documented in the notes (1-slope =
     6.2e-4 / 1.8e-3 / 5.8e-3 at dp = 1e-3 / 3e-3 / 1e-2). This is a separate
     effect from the orbit truncation and is currently unaddressed.
